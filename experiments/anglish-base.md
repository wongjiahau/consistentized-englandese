# Experiment: CE on an Anglish base (Option B)

Status: experiment, not part of the specification.

This file translates the example texts of SPEC.md again. It keeps every CE affix rule and the verb slots (with `-must`, Q18). It changes P4: the test is the history of the word, not only the affixes that a reader can see.

`experiments/anglish-ce.md` is an older run with looser rules. It kept everyday loans with no visible affix, revived old words and used the older grammar.

## Rules used

- **Native words stay.** A word is native if it comes from Old English or Old Norse, or if it came into Old English from Latin before 1066 (*street*, *wine*, *cheese*, *turn*).
- **Every other loan** from Latin, Greek or French is replaced, also when it has no affix that a reader can see (*machine*, *safe*, *joint*). Try these steps in order:
  1. **Part swap** to native roots, if a reader can guess the meaning from the parts (P4): *govern* (Greek *kubernan*, "steer") → *steer*; *parliament* (French *parler*, "speak") → *speakery*. A part swap that fails the guess test is skipped: *oppose* → ~~*antiset*~~.
  2. **Synonym swap** to a living native word with the same meaning: *large* → *big*; *cover* → *lid*.
  3. **Compound or derivation** from living native words: *Ground-Law*, *healery*.
  4. **Modern loan.** If steps 1–3 give nothing, or give only a form that is longer and harder than a short common loan, keep the modern loan as one base word: *machine*, *seal*, *vote*, *tax*, *person*. It then takes CE affixes only: *voteorage*, not *electorate*.
- **No revival.** Do not bring back a dead word (*thane*, *steven*, *geld*, *inwit*). Use the modern word, even if it is a loan (step 4).
- **Exempt:** the CE affixes themselves (`-ment`, `-ize`, `trans-`, `sub-`, …), P6 names (IUPAC, SI, species, trade names), and proper names (*Congress*, *Senate*, *London*).

Each table lists only the words that differ from the current CE text. The step column gives the step used: 1 part swap, 2 synonym swap, 3 compound or derivation, 4 modern loan kept.

## F. Universal Declaration of Human Rights

### Article 1

> All human bements bearen free and even in worthyness and rights. Thons bereasonen and beconsciencen and interdealbest in a spirit of brotherness.

- *equal* → *even* (2); *interact* → *interdeal* (2).
- *human*, *reason*, *conscience*, *spirit* stay (4). *wit* is not the same as *reason*, and *inwit* is a revival.

### Article 2

> Everyone betitlen to all the rights and freenesses setted forth in this Sayion, without distinguishment of any kind, such as race, hue, sex, tongue, religion, political or other belief, landal or societyal root, ownee, bearment or other standness. Furthermore, no distinguishment makenmust on the ground of the political, lawsayal or interlandal standness of the land or territory to which a person belong, whether it be selfstandent, trust, unselfsteering or under any other limitment of overlordness.

- *Declareion* → *Sayion* (3, `-ion`: what saying makes); *colour* → *hue*; *language* → *tongue*; *opinion* → *belief*; *origin* → *root*; *basis* → *ground*; *country* → *land* (all 2).
- *national* → *landal*, *international* → *interlandal*, *status* → *standness*, *undependent* → *selfstandent* (as German *selbständig*), *unselfgoverning* → *unselfsteering*, *sovereignness* → *overlordness* (all 3).
- *jurisdictional* → *lawsayal* (1: *ius* "law" + *dicere* "say").
- Stay (4): *title*, *race*, *sex*, *religion*, *political*, *societyal*, *person*, *territory*, *trust*, *limitment*, *distinguishment*. *sunder* would fit *distinguish*, but it is literary, close to a revival.

### Article 3

> Everyone have the right to life, freeness and safeness of person.

- *secureness* → *safeness*. *safe* is a loan too, but it is short and common (4). *secure* (*se-* + *cura*) has a visible affix.

### Article 4

> No one holdenmust in slaveness or bondness; slaveizement and the slave-trade forbidenmust in all their shapes.

- *serveorness* → *bondness* (2); *prohibitenmust* → *forbidenmust* (2); *forms* → *shapes* (2).
- *slave* stays (4). *thrall* is literary.

### Article 5

> No one subjectenmust to torture or to heartless, unhuman or lowizing handlement or punishment.

- *cruel* → *heartless* (2); *degrading* → *lowizing* (1: *de-* + *gradus*, "step down"); *treatment* → *handlement* (2).
- *subject*, *torture*, *punishment* stay (4).

## G. Constitution of the United States

### Preamble

> I'all the Folk of the United States, in Order to shape a wholer Oneness, set up Rightness, see to homeal Stillness, look after the shared Wardment, further the overall Welfare, and safeize the Blessions of Freeness to myself'all and my'all Offspringage, do lay down and set up this Ground-Law for the United States of America.

- *People* → *Folk*; *form* → *shape*; *establish* → *set up*; *Justness* → *Rightness*; *sureize* → *see to*; *Tranquilness* → *Stillness*; *provide for* → *look after*; *common* → *shared*; *promote* → *further*; *general* → *overall*; *ordain* → *lay down* (all 2).
- *perfecter Uniteion* → *wholer Oneness* (2, 3); *defendment* → *Wardment* (2); *secure* → *safeize* (4 + `-ize`); *Descendorage* → *Offspringage* (3: all offspring as a group).
- *Constituteion* → *Ground-Law* (3, as German *Grundgesetz*). A short compound of living words beats a long loan.

### Article I, Section 1

> All lawizive Powers herein gived lieizenmust in a Congress of the United States, which makenmust of a Senate and House of Representors.

- *granted* → *gived* (2); *vestenmust* → *lieizenmust* (3: make lie in); *consistmust of* → *makenmust of* (2: is made of).
- *Congress* and *Senate* are proper names. *Powers* and *Representors* stay (4).

## Findings

1. **Short common loans stay, and most of them have no affix.** Step 4 keeps *machine*, *seal*, *joint*, *vote*, *tax*, *rent*, *lease*. The words that change are mostly learned words with Latin affixes (*inspect*, *component*, *survive*, *replace*, *corruption*). In practice the rule is close to "no affixed loans", which was the first intent.
2. **Technical text stays easy to read** (A, B, H). Legal text keeps most of its core (*lease*, *rent*, *deposit*, *religion*, *political*), so it changes less than in `anglish-ce.md`.
3. **CE affixes do the work that Latin affixes did.** `-ery` (*healery*, *speakery*, *dwellery*), `-or` + `-age` (*steerorage*, *voteorage*, *Offspringage*), `-ion` (*tellion*, *mendion*, *findion*, *wrapion*), `be-` (*beguilt*, *bestrength*), `trans-` (*translive*, *Transthinkor*), `-ent` (*flowent*, *selfstandent*). No new affix was needed.
4. **One verb can replace many loans.** *inlook* replaces *inspect*, *examine* and *check*. This is good for P2, but it loses some difference in meaning.
5. **Some swaps make the meaning narrower:** *rust* (*corrosion*), *dwellery* (*premises*), *exengineoress* (*retired engineer*), *land* (*arrive*).
6. **The hard cases are judgment calls:**
   - Is *sunder* archaic? Is *thrall*?
   - Is *wit* the same as *reason*?
   - When is a compound "longer and harder" than the loan?

   These need a dictionary decision (§6), as in `anglish-ce.md`. There are fewer of them, because revivals are gone.
7. **A writer still needs word history to apply the rule.** A reader cannot see that *safe*, *easy* and *joint* are loans. Step 4 keeps the short ones anyway, so the error cost is low. The words that change are mostly the ones with a visible Latin affix.
